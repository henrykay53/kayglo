Two homes can get the same panels from the same manufacturer and end up with completely different results. The equipment is rarely what separates a premium installation from an average one. The difference is in the parts you can't see on a spec sheet.

## Engineering before installation

A good install starts weeks before anyone touches the roof.

- **A real shade study** — modeling how trees, chimneys, and neighboring rooflines affect production across the seasons, not a glance at the roof.
- **Structural analysis** — confirming your roof can carry the array safely for decades.
- **Right-sizing** — designing to your actual usage instead of the biggest system that fits.

Skip this and you get a system that looks fine on day one and underperforms for twenty years.

## Craftsmanship you can see

The visible details are where corners get cut on a rushed job — and where quality announces itself.

- **Concealed conduit** instead of pipe running down your exterior wall.
- **All-black, flush-mounted hardware** that sits low and quiet against the roof.
- **Clean wire management** with nothing sagging or exposed.
- **Meticulous cleanup** — a site left better than it was found.

> A solar array should look like it was always meant to be there. If it looks bolted on as an afterthought, it was.

## Accountability you can count on

A 25-year workmanship warranty is only as good as the company standing behind it. Ask any installer a simple question: *if my production dips in year eight, who do I call, and how fast will you come?* The answer tells you more than any brochure.
