It's the first question everyone asks, and the honest answer is: *it depends on what you want to power.* Anyone who quotes you a flat price before understanding your home hasn't done their job. But the things that move the price are simple to understand — and once you know them, you can tell a fair quote from a rushed one.

## What actually determines your price

Four things decide the size of your system, and therefore your cost:

- **How much you want to run.** Lights, fans, TV, and a fridge is a very different system from one running air conditioners, a borehole pump, and a deep freezer.
- **For how long.** Powering essentials through the night needs more battery than covering a few evening hours.
- **Battery capacity.** This is often the biggest single cost — and the part people are most tempted to cut, usually to their regret.
- **Quality of the equipment.** Genuine, warranty-backed panels, inverters, and batteries cost more than the counterfeits flooding the market — because they actually last.

## Why the cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest system

It's tempting to go with the lowest number. But a system built on substandard batteries and a weak inverter often fails within a year — and then you're paying twice.

> The most expensive solar system is the cheap one you have to replace. Buy genuine once, or buy again and again.
