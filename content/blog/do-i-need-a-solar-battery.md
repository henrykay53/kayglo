Adding a battery to a solar system is one of the most satisfying upgrades you can make to a home — and also one of the easiest to oversell. The right answer depends on three things: your utility's rate structure, how often your grid goes down, and what you want the battery to do.

## What a battery is actually for

There are three distinct jobs a home battery can do, and they matter to different homeowners in different amounts.

- **Backup power.** When the grid goes down, a battery keeps your essentials — or your whole home — running. If you experience regular outages or run critical equipment, this alone can justify the cost.
- **Bill optimization.** On time-of-use rates, a battery stores cheap or self-produced daytime energy and spends it during expensive evening peaks.
- **Self-reliance.** Storing your own solar means drawing less from the grid, which some homeowners value on principle.

## When storage clearly makes sense

- You're on a **time-of-use rate** with a steep evening peak.
- Your area sees **frequent or long outages**.
- Your utility offers **limited or declining net metering**, so exporting excess solar is worth less than storing it.

## When it can wait

If you have generous net metering, a stable grid, and a flat rate, a battery may be more about peace of mind than economics — and that's a perfectly valid reason, as long as you're choosing it with eyes open.

> The best battery decision is a clear-eyed one. We'd rather tell you to skip storage today and add it later than sell you capacity you won't use.

## Sizing matters more than brand

A battery that's too small won't carry you through an outage; one that's too large is money parked on a wall. We size storage to the loads you actually want to protect — refrigeration, medical devices, a home office, or the whole house — and design it to expand later if your needs grow.
