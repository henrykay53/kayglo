For years, the generator has been the default answer to Nigeria's power problem. It's familiar, and the upfront cost feels manageable. But a generator doesn't stop asking for money the day you buy it — and when you add everything up, it's often the far more expensive way to keep your lights on.

## The true cost of a generator

The purchase price is only the beginning. Over its life, a generator quietly drains far more:

- **Fuel.** Petrol or diesel, week after week, at whatever the pump charges that month.
- **Servicing.** Oil changes, filters, and regular maintenance to keep it running.
- **Repairs.** Parts wear out. Eventually the whole unit does too.
- **The hidden costs.** The noise, the fumes, the downtime when it won't start, and the wear on the appliances it powers.

Add a few years of that together and the number is usually eye-opening.

## How solar changes the maths

Solar asks for more upfront and then almost nothing after. Once it's installed:

> The money you were pouring into fuel and servicing every month stays in your pocket. Instead of feeding a machine, your system is quietly paying you back.

For many homes and businesses, the savings cover the cost of the system within a few years. After that, your power is essentially free for the rest of the system's life — and it's silent, clean, and always on.

## You don't have to choose overnight

Many of our clients keep their generator purely as an emergency backup while solar and batteries carry the day-to-day load. The generator goes from running every day to barely running at all — and the fuel bill falls off a cliff.

## The bottom line

A generator feels cheaper because you pay for it slowly, forever. Solar costs more once and then pays you back. Over any real length of time, it's not a close contest.
