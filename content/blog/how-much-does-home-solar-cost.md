The most common question we hear is also the hardest to answer in one number: *what does solar cost?* The honest reply is that it depends on your home — but the factors that move the price are knowable, and once you understand them you can spot a fair quote from a padded one.

## The five things that set your price

Solar pricing comes down to a handful of variables. Everything else is noise.

- **System size (kW).** The single biggest driver. A system is sized to your annual electricity usage, your roof, and your goals. Bigger isn't better — it's more expensive and often unnecessary.
- **Equipment tier.** Premium monocrystalline panels and quality inverters cost more up front and produce more, for longer, with better warranties.
- **Roof complexity.** Steep pitches, multiple planes, tile roofs, and shading all add labor and engineering.
- **Electrical work.** Older homes may need a panel upgrade to safely add solar or a battery.
- **Add-ons.** Battery storage and EV charging are separate line items with their own value.

## What you should actually compare

Comparing quotes on total price alone is how homeowners get burned. Two numbers matter far more:

1. **Price per watt** — the great equalizer across different system sizes.
2. **Net cost after incentives** — what you actually pay once credits are applied.

> A cheaper price per watt often hides thinner panels, a weaker warranty, or a crew that will be impossible to reach in five years. The lowest bid and the best value are rarely the same quote.

## Incentives that lower your net cost

The federal residential clean energy credit remains the largest lever for most homeowners, and many states and utilities layer their own incentives on top. Because these programs change and depend on your tax situation, treat any savings figure in a sales pitch as an estimate until it's specific to *you*.
