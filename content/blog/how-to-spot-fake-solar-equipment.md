If you've ever bought a battery or inverter that promised years of service and died within months, you're not alone. Nigeria's solar market is full of counterfeit and substandard equipment — relabelled batteries, fake capacity ratings, and inverters that can't handle the load they claim. Knowing what to look for can save you a lot of money and frustration.

## The red flags

Be cautious when you see any of these:

- **A price that's too good to be true.** Genuine batteries and inverters have a floor price. If one seller is far cheaper than everyone else, ask why.
- **No real warranty.** Genuine products come with a manufacturer's warranty and a dealer who will honour it. "Trust me" is not a warranty.
- **No documentation.** Missing datasheets, no serial numbers, or labels that look reprinted are warning signs.
- **Unknown or "copycat" brands.** Names that look almost like a well-known brand are often deliberate imitations.
- **Vague specifications.** If a seller can't clearly state the battery's real capacity and cycle life, they may not know — or may not want you to know.

## Why genuine equipment matters

This isn't about paying more for a badge. Genuine equipment is a safety and value issue:

> A quality lithium battery can deliver thousands of charge cycles over many years. A counterfeit one may give you a few hundred before it swells, fails, or becomes a fire risk.

Real panels hold their output for two decades. Real inverters protect your appliances instead of frying them during an outage. The difference isn't visible in the shop — it shows up a year later.

## How to protect yourself

- **Buy from a reputable, established dealer** who will still be there when you need support.
- **Insist on genuine, warranty-backed products** and keep the paperwork.
- **Ask for the exact specifications** and confirm they match your needs.
- **Be wary of pressure to "buy now"** at a suspiciously low price.
